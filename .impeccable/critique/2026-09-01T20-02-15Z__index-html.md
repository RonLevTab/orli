## Design Specificity Verdict

**Authored in the argument. Borrowed in the composition.** Not category-interchangeable SaaS — but not yet a Hebrew clinic-software page either. It is a well-written Hebrew argument wearing a Latin editorial template.

**LLM assessment.** What is genuinely specific: `#compare` (index.html:186–201) makes ten claims and every one is a mechanism claim, not an adjective. The FAQ leads with the real objection ("יש לנו כבר אופטימה, בשביל מה צריך את אורלי?"). The problem section opens on a scene, not a stat. Naming Optima by name throughout is the confident move and the page makes it.

Where it goes interchangeable: the hero is a stock AI-SaaS aurora. `hero-gradient.js` is by its own header comment a reimplementation of the GradFlow component, rendering mint `#98e2d6` and **lilac `#beaae4`** — lilac is not in the brand. The largest surface on the site, the first thing a clinic owner sees, is a colour field belonging to no part of this product's identity. The type system is Latin with Hebrew substituted in: `text-transform: uppercase` (a no-op in Hebrew) survives, and Latin display tracking survives on six label styles — `.faq-tag` at 10.24px/**20% letter-spacing**, `.cmp-th` 18%, `.scrolly-idx` 12%. At 20% tracking Hebrew letterforms visibly disconnect. `.eyebrow` alone got the corrective (styles.css:70, down to 2%); five siblings kept the Latin value. Roughly 17 unused rule families remain in styles.css (`.hiw-*` glow cards, `.steps`, `.feature-grid`, `.compare-col`…) — the fossil of a features-grid SaaS template the real content outgrew.

The missed opportunity is singular: **the clinic owner never sees her own screen.** The pitch is "your Optima calendar, opened to patients," and `#demo` shows only the patient's half. No split view, no Optima day-sheet filling in. That is the one image no competitor can truthfully make, and the page argues for it entirely in words while spending its largest visual budget on a lilac noise field.

## Overall Impression

The writing on this site is better than the design around it, and the gap is the story. Someone thought hard about the argument — the objection-first FAQ, the mechanism-only compare table, "אורלי לא מחליפה את המזכירה. היא משחררת אותה מהטלפון" — and then dressed it in a template whose hero belongs to another company. The single biggest opportunity is not a fix, it's a substitution: replace the borrowed aurora with the one picture this product owns, the clinic's own Optima calendar filling itself in.

The second story is that the interactive proof is quietly broken in three separate ways, and each is a small fix: the widget resets on scroll, the card is one-fifth empty because of a dead CSS gate, and the success scene it all builds toward has no buttons at all.

## What's Working

1. **`#compare` persuades typographically, not with badges.** styles.css:509–519 gives the "with" column `--ink` at weight 500 while "without" stays `--ink-soft` at 400, plus a 6% teal wash and a hairline divider. The reader feels which side is better before reading a word — and every row is a verifiable mechanism claim. This section alone earns PRODUCT.md's positioning.
2. **The Hebrew RTL craft is above field standard where it was applied.** styles.css:154–179 hand-mirrors the hero glow and rewrites the veil's gradient direction, with the reasoning in the comment, because decorative physical insets don't auto-flip. `widget.js` uses `<bdi>` and per-span `dir` so the bidi algorithm never reorders phone numbers or prices. Most Hebrew SaaS sites set `dir="rtl"` and ship.
3. **The widget replica's source-mapping discipline.** widget.js:1–35 maps every block to the file it mirrors in `orli-calendar/widget/src/`. PRODUCT.md Principle 3 makes demo fidelity a marketing requirement; this is the mechanism that makes that principle survivable rather than aspirational.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] The demo form promises contact and sends nothing
**What.** script.js:14–28 validates client-side, prints "תודה. ניצור קשר עם {clinic}…", then calls `form.reset()`. There is no endpoint.
**Why it matters.** PRODUCT.md defines success as a clinic owner submitting this form. Every submission on the site's only conversion goal is silently discarded while the user is told the opposite — and the reset means they cannot even copy what they typed to send it manually. This also contradicts the project's own Principle 4, "say only what's shipped."
**Fix.** Either wire it (form endpoint, or `mailto:` as a stopgap), or stop claiming contact: state that submission opens their mail client, and put a real address, phone, or WhatsApp link permanently under the button. Never `reset()` before a send is confirmed.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### [P1] Scrolling destroys any interaction with the demo widget
**What.** scrolly.js's IntersectionObserver calls `activate(i)` → `setScene(i)` → `reset()`. Verified live: text typed into the patient step was discarded and the widget returned to "בחרו תאריך" on a single scroll nudge.
**Why it matters.** index.html:115 says "נסו את התוסף בעצמכם" — *try it yourself*. The page invites the interaction, then confiscates it on the next trackpad twitch. PRODUCT.md says this widget carries more persuasive weight than the copy around it; on desktop it is not actually usable.
**Fix.** On the first real interaction inside the widget, disconnect the step observer for the session and invert the relationship — let `widget.onStep` drive the step list, which scrolly.js already implements. Scroll narrative for passive readers; the moment someone touches it, they drive.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] Mobile has no navigation, and the mobile hero buries the pitch
**What.** styles.css:422 sets `.nav-links { display: none }` at ≤920px with no hamburger or replacement anywhere in the codebase. On all four pages a phone visitor gets a wordmark and one CTA — no route to `#demo`, `#compare`, `#faq`, or integration.html. Simultaneously `.hero-visual { order: -1 }` promotes an `aria-hidden`, `pointer-events:none` widget picture above the H1, and the hero measures 854px against an 844px viewport, so the lead's closing line falls below the fold.
**Why it matters.** This audience evaluates on a phone between patients. They arrive, see a fake booking card whose prominent teal button does nothing when tapped, scroll past it to reach the headline, and have no menu to go anywhere.
**Fix.** Ship a mobile menu, even a simple disclosure listing the four anchors. Move `.hero-visual` below `.hero-copy` on mobile or crop it to a peek. Trim hero padding so the copy fits inside `100dvh`.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`

### [P1] The demo card is one-fifth empty and hides its own primary button
**What.** `.demo-browser` is 660px tall; `.obw-widget` is 540px — 120px of blank cream under every scene. The cause: the 27 compaction rules at widget.css:45–74 are all gated on `[data-scrolly]`, and **nothing in the repo ever sets that attribute**. So the confirm scene renders full-size, `.obw-step` scrolls internally with a visible scrollbar, and "אישור הזמנה" sits below the widget's own fold — beneath which is an eighth of a card of nothing.
**Why it matters.** The demo is the proof. It currently reads as a broken embed: dead space below, cut-off content above, a scrollbar in a card that has room to spare.
**Fix.** Set `data-scrolly` on the `#demo` mount in `widget.js`'s `init()` (not the hero mockup), or delete the dead ruleset and size `.demo-browser` to the widget's real height. One line either way.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`

### [P2] Contrast fails the AA floor PRODUCT.md commits to, on the most-repeated elements
**What.** `--teal` #0f8a86 on cream = **3.85:1** (detector independently: 3.9:1) — the colour of every `.eyebrow` on all four pages, plus `.scrolly-idx` and `.rung-tag`. CTA placeholders **2.36:1**; CTA input borders **1.68:1** (fails the 3:1 non-text minimum); the compare table's ✕ #c98b7f = **2.76:1**.
**Why it matters.** PRODUCT.md names WCAG AA as the working floor, so these are commitments not met. The ✕ matters twice: under 640px it becomes the only signal distinguishing the two comparison columns.
**Fix.** Use a darker teal for small text — `--teal-deep` #0a5d5a is already defined and clears 4.5:1 on cream. Raise CTA placeholder to `rgba(255,255,255,.78)` and borders to `.55`. Darken the ✕ and pair it with a text label on mobile.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable audit`

## Minor Observations

- **Roadmap shipped-vs-coming is invisible** — `.rung.soon` (styles.css:329) already defines the muted grey recipe this needs and is used nowhere. Give "בקרוב" the muted treatment and a dashed top rule. Nearly P1 given the honesty stakes.
- **`--bg` is undefined.** styles.css:114 uses `color-mix(in srgb, var(--bg) 86%, transparent)` on `.nav`; `--bg` appears nowhere, so the declaration is invalid and the sticky header computes to transparent. Almost certainly meant `var(--cream)`.
- **Two `<h1>`s on index.html** — `widget.js` emits `<h1 class="obw-title">` per step, correct for a standalone embed, wrong mounted mid-document. Matches the detector's `skipped-heading` findings.
- **Focusable controls inside `aria-hidden="true"`** — widget.js wraps the language button *and* the progress bar in an `aria-hidden` div. The button stays keyboard-operable (WCAG 4.1.2) and the only "which step am I on" signal is hidden from assistive tech.
- **All four pages ship wordmark `<img>` without `width`/`height`** (detector-only finding) — avoidable layout shift on every page load.
- **`.ladder-note` renders synthetic-italic Hebrew**, contradicting the rule and rationale four lines above it in the same stylesheet.
- **No copy button on the embed snippet** — integration.html has zero `<button>` elements. The IT contact's whole job on that page is hand-copying a four-line snippet out of a `direction: ltr` `<pre>` inside an RTL document.
- **Bidi scrambles technical prose on integration.html** — `direction:ltr` chips whose *sequence* still follows RTL, so dot-separated lists read backwards and "ו־" attaches to the wrong chip.
- **`cdn.orli.health`** (integration.html:81) is a domain the rest of the site never mentions. Verify against reality.
- **privacy.html uses teal ✓ check-marks to list data collected about the patient** — a benefit affordance carrying a disclosure. Use a neutral bullet or definition list.
- **"וּויתר"** (vav with dagesh) at index.html:199 where "וויתר" is intended.
- **~17 unused rule families in styles.css** — harmless at runtime, corrosive when someone next tries to find the real system.

## Questions to Consider

1. **Why does the clinic owner never see her own screen?** What would `#demo` look like as a split — patient widget on one side, an Optima day-sheet filling in on the other?
2. **What is the lilac for?** If the brand is cream, teal and dark-green ink, what argument does a mint-and-lilac WebGL field make that a still, confident cream hero with the mechanism drawn in it would not make better?
3. **If the demo invites interaction, why does the page fight it?** Right now you pay the full engineering cost of both a scroll narrative and a usable widget, and deliver neither cleanly. Pick one.
4. **What does the hero ask the reader to do?** There is no CTA in the hero — no button, no link, no scroll cue. On a page whose success metric is form submissions, what is the first screen for?
5. **What would an honest form look like while the backend does not exist?** "אין לנו עדיין טופס — כתבו לנו ל־X" would be less polished and more trustworthy, and this product's positioning is built on trustworthiness.
6. **The team fixed Hebrew letter-spacing in exactly one selector.** What else in this stylesheet is a Latin default nobody has re-asked in Hebrew yet?
